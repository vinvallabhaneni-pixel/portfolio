Korth & Shannahan:
Paid Search, Web,
and Customer Data

I run digital marketing for a family-owned painting and carpentry contractor serving Westchester County. The work centers on Google Ads campaign management, supported by the website, the customer database, search visibility, email, and social, all built so that each piece sends the right homeowners to the same place.

Company
Korth & Shannahan Painting and Carpentry
Role
Digital Marketing Specialist
Industry
Residential Painting and Carpentry, Westchester NY
Focus
Google Ads, CMS, CRM, SEO, Social, Email
Background

The situation

Korth & Shannahan is a family-owned painting and finish carpentry company that has worked in Westchester since 1979. Its projects are large and high-value, so the goal was never broad traffic. It was leads from the right towns, tracked back to the work that produced them.

Three constraints shaped the approach. The website runs on a managed WordPress theme that cannot be edited directly. The customer list had grown to thousands of contacts with uneven data. And search visibility was far below what a business of this size should have, with very few clicks for the number of impressions.

I started with paid search and the data underneath it, then rebuilt the pages those campaigns and emails send people to.

Work

What I run

01
Google Ads Campaign Management
Built and manage the company's Google Ads campaign. Instead of bidding across all of Westchester, the campaign is aimed at a short list of high-value towns, including Scarsdale, Chappaqua, Larchmont, Pound Ridge, Armonk, Rye, and Bronxville, because the projects the company wants are large and the budget has to go where those homeowners are. Each campaign points to a dedicated landing page on its own subdomain rather than the general site, so the page can match the ad. The Google Ads conversion tag and call tracking are carried across the rebuilt pages so a phone call can be traced to the campaign that produced it. I also manage account access and user permissions, and I own the marketing budget the campaign runs on.
02
CMS and Website Work
Rebuilt the homepage, Customer HQ, Areas Served, and Project Gallery pages for a site that runs on a managed WordPress theme with no direct theme access. Each page is a self-contained Custom HTML widget in Elementor, so it can be pasted in and updated without touching theme files. The live header and blog were restyled sitewide through one Additional CSS file built on brand variables, so every page picks up the change at once. The Customer HQ page now opens with the FAQ and the company's commitments, and the gallery uses a photo lightbox on a brand-color background.
03
CRM and Customer List Management
Took a contact export of 5,757 records and sorted it by engagement into five groups: active in the last 12 months, lukewarm, cold, never engaged, and new. That produced a 4,005-contact keep list and a 1,726-contact win-back list, with 26 records set aside for data problems such as two addresses in one field. Separately, 2,209 contacts had a full name typed into the first name field with the last name blank. I split those automatically and flagged the 64 ambiguous cases, such as couples or business names, for a manual decision instead of guessing. I also flagged that bounced and unsubscribed contacts need a separate export and should never be mailed again.
04
Email Marketing and List Segmentation
Designed the seasonal email campaigns in table-based HTML that holds up across email clients: a fall offer pairing a full exterior project with a free day of interior painting, a follow-up for homeowners who still want exterior work done before winter, and a later-season discount send. Sends are matched to the segments above, so engaged customers get the offer and the win-back list is handled separately from the keep list.
05
SEO
Ran a full-site audit that included a 35-page inventory, a broken-link log, and migration notes for any future redesign. The Search Console report showed 31,061 impressions producing 98 clicks, a 0.32 percent click-through rate, and separated two different problems. The carpentry page earned over 12,000 impressions but ranked around page four, which is a ranking problem. A blog post ranking first for a molding term earned no clicks, which points to either a weak snippet or searchers who were never going to hire. That split decided what to fix first, and it led to a town-level landing page template for the high-intent local searches the site was barely ranking for.
06
Social Media
Support the company's social presence using the same project photography, seasonal offers, and messaging that run on the website and in email, so a homeowner sees one consistent story across every channel.
Systems

How the pieces connect

Each channel feeds the others. Ads send homeowners to purpose-built pages, call tracking shows which campaign earned the call, search data decides which town pages to build next, and the segmented list decides who receives which email.

Paid Search
Google Ads + Call Tracking
Town-targeted campaign, dedicated landing page, conversion tag, and call tracking so leads trace back to the campaign.
Website
WordPress + Elementor + CSS
Custom HTML widgets for core pages and one sitewide stylesheet for the header and blog, built around a theme that cannot be edited directly.
Customer Data
Segmented CRM List
Engagement-based groups, a keep list, a win-back list, and name fields repaired before any send.
SEO
Search Console + Page Inventory
Page-level and query-level analysis that separates ranking problems from snippet problems and sets the order of fixes.
